# 1a1b4-melody-harmonization

A melody can be conceived and harmonized in two ways (within Ancient Mode):

1\. **A melody derived exclusively from the Ancient Scale**, which is simply harmonized within itself; that is, no tones beyond those belonging to the melody itself exist.  This includes tones outside the scale that form part of the melody.

2\. **A "free" melody** (a "Cherubic chant," for instance), in which the tonal center of the scale shifts according to the availability of pitches.  Various kinds of modulation are possible here, encompassing more or fewer of the available pitches.  Modulation may involve only a handful of pitches, or it may be *diatonic*—that is, it may also include those pitches that are common to both scales.

In connection with this, it is possible to create a harmonic texture based on **mixture harmonization**.  This means:

- Harmonization arises through mixtures.
- The harmonic texture is interwoven with movements that are, in essence, mixture movements.  These are entirely identical to the mixture movements found in ordinary classical-tonal harmony.

The vocal principles of choral (SATB) writing may also have the following characteristics:

- **Unison**
- **Mixture** (2:2, or 4, or 3:1, or 1:3, or another configuration when more pitches are involved, such as 3:2)
- **Accents**, as well as isolated unaccented notes
- **Ornamentation** in the style of [AsDi](../3-opus/Ascetic_Discourse.md)
- **Dynamics of the melody**: certain notes of the melody are emphasized, while others disappear.
