## The Icon
by Djuro Zivkovic

_The Icon_ (for cello and piano, or cello solo) is written in 2026 for the distinguished Serbian cellist Petar Pejčić. The commission came shortly after our collaboration on the performance of my cello concerto _Unceasing Prayers_ with the BBC Symphony Orchestra in London in 2024. I accepted the invitation with great joy. Petar's interpretation of my music revealed rare qualities: imagination, intensity, concentration, power, and a profound sense of artistic purpose. Alongside classical repertoire, his engagement with contemporary music is of a rare sophistication: he combines exceptional technical command with a strongly individual vision, and I am convinced that his artistry will remain one of the significant voices in decades to come.

The title of the work derives from a central concept within my compositional system, **The Ancient Mode**. At its core stands the idea of the **Icon-theme**: a self-sufficient and archetypal melody that emerges through the internal logic of the system itself. In this understanding, a musical theme functions as a symbolic object, analogous to a sacred icon.

The piece is composed in a tripartite form (A–B–A¹) and may be understood as a meditation on the Icon-theme through a series of variations. The first large section (A) presents the theme and its symbolic foundations. Among the compositional techniques used, within the Ancient Mode, is the encoding of names into music. For instance, the cello unfolds a melodic structure derived from the name "IESUS CHRISTUS", generated as a geometric realization on the Ancient Mode matrix. Simultaneously, the piano presents the name "THEOTOKOS". Its rhythmic structure is determined through Morse code, while its harmonic content comes from visualizing the letters as bitmap forms projected onto the matrix. This opening section represents our encounter with the sacred icon itself, in several stages. We stand before it in contemplation; we gaze into it and find ourselves gazed upon in return, discovering a living presence within.

The central section (B) resembles the character of my double concerto for cello and piano. Its material is built around the idea of an "impossible melody": a melodic line that strives toward the highest degree of melodic richness while remaining beyond the limits of singing and immediate comprehension, and so the listener follows a musical discourse that continually exceeds expectation. The rapid chordal formations in the piano are derived from the same melodic source, compressed simultaneously in both pitch and time. This part of the music leads beyond the visible surface of the icon, toward the threshold of the metaphysical, into an untouchable realm, yet fully present.

The final section (A¹) brings a revelation. It is a sublime dwelling within the icon’s deepest interior, in the fulfillment of all things, abiding in pure Love, illuminated from within. At the conclusion of the music, the Icon-theme appears in a five-voiced unison melodic texture shared between the performers, which symbolizes humanity united in a single voice, gathered around one image, one one act of love, and truth.

The composition is dedicated to Petar Pejčić.
