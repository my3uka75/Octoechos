The mystery is this: how did Bach draw his combinations out of the Infinite itself? Or more:
how did Baroque counterpoint, whole and luminous in its sublime architecture, step forth from the Infinite?

If we behold the totality of all that is possible, with singularity at one pole and total entropy at the other, we arrive at this conclusion: a single Bach fugue, one among all the Bach fugues, which are a subset of all of Bach's compositions, which in turn are a subset of all Baroque music of its kind, the wellspring from which all music of that kind flows, occupies, on this scale between singularity and entropy, an exceedingly narrow sliver of possibility. And yet, however small a fragment it is against the whole of what could be, to us the living, bound by time and space, it stands for a nearly infinite number of Baroque compositions.

Thus, let us conceive of a polyphonic composition comprising three voices, spanning fifty measures in common time (4/4). It is chromatic in nature. It is put in the range of the German harpsichord, from 1F to f3. The shortest rhythmic value, whether note or rest, is 1/16, and the longest is 1/1. We use grid of 16, incremental between these rhythmic values.
The question is: *What is the total number of combinations using these parameters; how many pieces we can generate?*
### Calculation
- For one voice:
$$
g(800) \approx 5.43 \times 10^{1410}.
$$
- For three voices:
$$
G_3(800) = (g(800))^3
$$
- Final:
$$
160.6 \times 10^{4230}
$$
### Answer
The total number of such polyphonic compositions that may be written within these parameters is 
$$
1.606 \times 10^{4232}
$$
For comparison, let the discerning mind consider that the total number of atoms within our universe is but 10^80; and let it be held in understanding that 10^81 is merely *ten times* that number, so that we may rightly grasp the depths of this abyss of the **Great Musical Art**.
