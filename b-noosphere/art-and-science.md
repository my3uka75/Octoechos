Both art and science strive toward the same end: the unveiling of truth and the dwelling in truth. Ontologically they have the same source, the source of identification with the divine powers, and reflection of it. 
Yet the difference between art and science in their mechanics and in their eschatology is profound. Science proceeds along “the one true and straight line,” advancing toward understanding---from lesser knowledge to greater, from the narrow knowing to the broad. On the other hand, **art has already attained unto total knowledge**; it stands, like particles of light, "at the end of time." Science is a stepwise journey, art is the immediate end. 

Thus we behold a scientist, a great human of the human history, Nikola Tesla, who did unveil hidden parts of science. **Yet those same truths of science would in our created time have been revealed**; they would have been disclosed to another living soul along the same time-path of humanity’s temporal knowing. **But the music of Bach could be begotten by Bach alone, and by no other**; for that particular corner of truth was unveiled by Bach alone. His music abides in the domain of love, faith, and hope, where the heart dwells in a mysterious infinity.

And so, by this, art stands upon a higher plane of knowledge than science: for it reaches the absolute truth even now and ever, and is timelessly united with the living Hypostasis. The art is an ultimate testament of the truth and the end time.

Science stands at an infinite distance from the Absolute, and  this infinity is permanent. For any span measured against the Infinite remains ever an infinite space. Yet science is not without meaning; for with each step of its progress, **it bears witness**, ever more clearly, **that God is possible**, and that **God is alive.**

In days to come---soon, if they be found worthy---men shall act with the very powers of Him who once wrought "wonders," for the marvels shall pass from the domain of mystery into the domain of what is possible, and the miracle shall clothe itself in the garments of reason.
