# There Are Two Kinds of Knowledge…

Human knowledge can be of two kinds: the first is intellectual, the second spiritual. Intellectual knowledge is the knowing of numbers; it is a logical system of links. Despite its complexity, the wisdom of logic is easy to learn and master, since intellectual knowledge is reasonable, readily accessible, and feasibly attainable, with regions of finite boundaries.

The logic of spirit is not link-knowledge, and so it cannot be learned in the same manner through the intellect, since spiritual knowledge is not about knowing numbers. Spiritual knowledge is the only sincere, indivisible, beyond-essential and beyond-infinite knowledge. It lies above all possible and impossible knowing: a deep, noetic cognition of being through a harmonious and intimate relationship with the eternal essence.

> “All spiritual contemplation should be governed by faith, hope and love, but most of all by love.” — St. Diadochos of Photiki

In the wisdom of spirit there can be no “gray zone” of progress, but rather an immediate knowing of the spirit. However, spiritual wisdom is far more complex than that of the intellect; its threads are interwoven into thousands of nodes, and it is difficult to master without the numerous obstacles and countless dangers that meet spiritual development.

All logical and spiritual knowledge can be applied to every human activity. Accordingly, productive-art knowledge is comparable to the general knowledge above, and consequently musical knowledge can be that of numbers and that of spirit.

The essence of today’s prevailing understanding of productive art (i.e., music composing) is a belief that the intellectual exertion of creation leads to great art, and hence to an understanding of Art as the knowing of numbers. This is a misconception. Not only artists but even the greatest scientists, such as Tesla, were aware of the spiritual domain of knowledge, and not merely of the intellect’s knowing of numbers. The frequent comparison with Pythagoras is mistaken. Truly yes, mathematics had the power to discriminate between mechanics and spirit. No golden section can be true, either, if it is only a mechanics of numbers.

Using automata-modeling in music creation can be a serious disadvantage if the soul’s incensive powers and spiritual energies are at a low level. Musical structures are a unified entity of low-level structures (numbers) and high-level structures (powers and energies), where the high structures are unknown to numbers. Yet some composers completely deny spiritual knowledge in favor of intellectual knowledge in their music creation: those who strictly obey the system of numbers and, within a complex number theory, ask for great Art. However, low-level mechanics (the system of numbers) can be total and unlimited only if it enlightens the high-level radiant energies of the absolute truth, beyond numbers. There is, therefore, no limitation in musical formalized structure. The serial concept, or any concept of automated organization of numbers that would go beyond the knowing of numbers, is extremely hard to achieve; but sadly, Western art, being unaware of this, intones a mantra over the dead body of mechanical creation.
